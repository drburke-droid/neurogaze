Binocular vision dysfunction (BVD), explained

Binocular vision dysfunction (BVD) is a common name for eyes that don't quite work together as a team. Your eyes can look straight and see 20/20, yet rest slightly out of line, so your brain has to hold them in line and correct them all day. That extra work is thought to contribute to headaches, eye strain, neck pain and tired eyes, especially after reading or screen time.

What BVD can feel like

People with BVD most often describe:

  • Headaches, especially after reading or screen time
  • Tired, sore or strained eyes, or a dry, gritty feeling
  • Neck and shoulder tension
  • Jaw tension or clenching
  • Losing your place, skipping lines or rereading when you read
  • Words that blur, double or seem to move after a while

Some people also notice dizziness or motion sickness, or feel overwhelmed in busy places like grocery stores.

These symptoms can have many causes. Measuring how your eyes line up, far and near, is how we find out whether BVD is part of the picture.

Why small misalignments can cause symptoms

1

The cause

To look at anything, both eyes have to point at exactly the same spot. For many people the eyes rest slightly out of line, so all day they have to be held in line and pulled back into line after every jump. The eye muscles report that extra work to the brain.

2

The effect

Those reports travel along the trigeminal nerve, which also carries feeling from the face and jaw and from the covering of the brain, and meets the nerves from the back of the head and neck in the brainstem. The extra traffic is thought to over-stimulate this nerve, which may show up as headaches, eye strain, jaw tension, neck pain, a dry-eye feeling or motion sickness.

Ways to treat it

Depending on what we find:

Prism glasses

Prism shifts what you see very slightly, so your eyes don't have to work as hard to line up. Neurolens is one type, with more prism toward the bottom of the lens, where you read.

Learn about Neurolens

The right prescription

Focusing and eye alignment are linked, so getting your glasses or contact lens prescription right for your day, screens included, is part of the picture.

Eye exercises, for some types

Some problems, such as difficulty turning the eyes in for close work (convergence insufficiency), can improve with eye exercises (vision therapy).

A referral, if needed

If something else could be behind your symptoms, we'll point you to the right person.
