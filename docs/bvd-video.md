# The BVD video

A 5-minute YouTube video that tells the BVD story in 3D, made from the same story as the website (`video.html` plays it from `video-script.js`). It comes in two versions, both with original music:

- **With an AI voice** (5:27): the narration is read by an open-source AI voice (Lily, British English), with short captions on screen.
- **Without a voice** (6:21): the full narration is on screen to read, so it works with the sound off (as most social video is watched). It runs a little longer to give time to read.

Everything is made here: the animation, the voice, the music, the caption file, the YouTube chapters and the thumbnails.

## The first 20 seconds

People decide in the first few seconds, so the video opens with *their* experience, then shows them something they've never seen:

1. **0:00** "Strained and tired eyes by the end of the day. Headaches. A tight jaw. A stiff neck." The head in profile; a small pulsing target appears on the eye, the forehead and temple, the jaw and the back of the neck, each as it's said.
2. **0:08** "Yet you see 20/20, and your eyes are healthy." The head turns to face us beside an eye chart with 20/20 ✓ and Healthy eyes ✓.
3. **0:12** "Cover one eye, and look behind the paddle. It has drifted out of line." Close on the eyes: the paddle covers one, turns see-through (a green scan sweeps down it), and the eye behind it has drifted.
4. **0:18** "When eyes rest out of line like this and it causes symptoms, it's often called binocular vision dysfunction, or BVD. Here's how it works, and a ten-second test you can try yourself." The title comes in on "binocular". The promised test comes at 1:40 (the red dot), which keeps people watching.

**Why "20/20" instead of "your eye doctor says your eyes are fine":** it keeps the same surprise without suggesting another doctor missed something, which viewers (and the College's advertising rules) could read as a criticism of colleagues. It's also exactly true: 20/20 measures how clearly you see, not how well your eyes line up.

## The cover test, step by step

The cover test chapter is built so viewers see what an examiner sees before they see the trick:

1. An ordinary, solid paddle moves from eye to eye, five times. Each time it leaves an eye, that eye makes a small movement back into line (after a fifth of a second to react, as a real eye does).
2. "So where was the eye?" The paddle turns see-through, and the covered eye is turned outward.
3. The paddle moves to the other eye: the newly covered eye drifts out and the uncovered one straightens.
4. Asleep: the eyelids close first, and only after a pause does the X-ray view show both eyes drifted to rest.

## The voice

The narration is made by [Kokoro](https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro), an open-source text-to-speech model (Apache 2.0 licence, free for commercial use), running on the computer that makes the video; nothing is sent anywhere. The voice is **bf_lily** (British English, female) at 1.1× speed, chosen for a brighter, peppier delivery. Others tried: af_heart, bm_george and bf_emma, and a peppier set (af_bella, af_jessica, af_kore, af_nova, af_sarah, af_sky, am_eric, am_liam, am_puck, bf_isabella, bm_lewis). To change it, run `tools/voiceover.py` with `--voice` (and `--speed`) and make the video again: a different voice speaks at a different pace, so the video is re-timed to it.

The voice says a few things differently from how they're written: "20/20" as "twenty twenty", "7.5" as "seven and a half", "250" as "two hundred and fifty", "15,000" as "fifteen thousand", the web address in words, "saccade" as sa-KADE (rhyming with arcade) and "proprioceptors" as pro-pree-oh-SEP-ters. These are in `SAY` in `video-script.js` and `PHONEME_FIXES` in `tools/voiceover.py`. An ellipsis (…) in the narration ends a phrase and the voice pauses a little longer after it. The narration is checked by transcribing it back with a speech-recognition model; every word came back as written (numbers come back as digits).

**On YouTube**, it's good practice to say the narration is an AI voice. The description below does. When uploading, YouTube asks whether the video has "altered or synthetic content"; that question is about realistic content that could mislead people (such as a real person's cloned voice), and a narrator's voice that isn't anyone's real voice is generally fine to answer no, but check YouTube's current guidance.

## The music

The music is generated from scratch by `tools/make-audio.py`, so there is nothing to license and no copyright claims. It moves along at 92 beats a minute: pads, a plucked arpeggio, a bass line, a soft kick, a shaker on the off-beats and a light snap. It follows the story: curious for the opening, warm while things are explained, darker for the symptoms, and brighter for what helps, where it resolves to a final chord. It dips automatically while the voice speaks, and the finished sound is evened out to YouTube's loudness (-14 LUFS), so YouTube won't turn it down. `--music` makes it louder or quieter (in dB).

## Making it again

You need Node with Playwright and Chromium, ffmpeg, and Python 3.10+ with `pip install kokoro-onnx soundfile numpy scipy`, plus Kokoro's model files (`kokoro-v1.0.onnx` and `voices-v1.0.bin`) from https://github.com/thewh1teagle/kokoro-onnx/releases/tag/model-files-v1.0. Serve the repository locally (for example `npx http-server -p 8080`), then:

```
# The voice (about a minute)
python tools/voiceover.py --models path/to/kokoro --out build/vo --voice bf_lily --speed 1.1

# The AI-voice version: record the picture timed to the voice, then add the voice and music
node tools/record-video.mjs --url http://localhost:8080/video.html --mode voice --voice build/vo/voice.json --out build/bvd-voice.mp4
python tools/make-audio.py --timeline build/bvd-voice.timeline.json --voice build/vo --video build/bvd-voice.mp4 --out bvd-video-ai-voice.mp4

# The version without a voice: narration on screen, music only
node tools/record-video.mjs --url http://localhost:8080/video.html --mode text --out build/bvd-text.mp4
python tools/make-audio.py --timeline build/bvd-text.timeline.json --video build/bvd-text.mp4 --out bvd-video-no-voice.mp4

# Thumbnails
node tools/thumbnails.mjs --url http://localhost:8080/ --out thumbs/

# A Short (red-dot, x-ray, asleep or mirror): its own voice, then the vertical picture, then the sound
python tools/voiceover.py --models path/to/kokoro --out build/vo-red-dot --voice bf_lily --speed 1.1 --short red-dot
node tools/record-video.mjs --url http://localhost:8080/video.html --format 9x16 --short red-dot --voice build/vo-red-dot/voice.json --out build/red-dot.mp4
python tools/make-audio.py --timeline build/red-dot.timeline.json --voice build/vo-red-dot --video build/red-dot.mp4 --out short-red-dot.mp4
```

The recorder also writes the captions (`.srt`) and chapters (`.chapters.txt`) beside the video. `--from` and `--to` record part of the video (shots by number, `--to` not included), and `--format 9x16` records vertically. It records one exact frame at a time, so the motion is smooth however slow the computer: with a graphics card that takes a few minutes; without one, set `SWIFTSHADER=1` and allow about an hour for each version. To preview without recording, open `video.html` in a browser and press **Play** (add `?mode=text` for the version without a voice, or `?format=9x16&short=red-dot` for a Short); it plays in real time, without sound.

If you change the narration in `video-script.js`, make the voice again before recording, so the timing matches.

## Accuracy notes

The video uses the website's wording rules: the link between eye alignment and symptoms is "thought to"; BVD is a common name rather than a single diagnosis ("it's often called"); normal eye-muscle signals are separated from the extra from holding and correcting misaligned eyes; "switches off" for saccadic suppression is a simplification of a large, brief drop in visual sensitivity; reading is shown at 250 words a minute, a typical adult pace, and "about 15,000 jumps an hour" counts one jump a word (real reading skips some words and goes back over others); and there are no comparisons with other practitioners.
