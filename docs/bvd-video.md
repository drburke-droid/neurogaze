# The BVD video

A 5½-minute YouTube video that tells the BVD story in 3D, made from the same story as the website (`video.html` plays it from `video-script.js`). You add your voice; the animation, on-screen captions, chapters, caption file and thumbnails are made here.

## The first 20 seconds

People decide in the first few seconds, so the video opens with *their* experience, then shows them something they've never seen:

1. **0:00** "You can see twenty-twenty, and your eyes are healthy." An eye chart with 20/20 ✓ and Healthy eyes ✓.
2. **0:04** "So why are they sore and strained by the end of every day? Why the headaches, the stiff neck, that wiped-out feeling?" Red glows on the eyes, forehead, temples and neck; each symptom pops up as you say it.
3. **0:14** "Watch this eye when it's covered. It drifts out of line." Close on the eyes: the paddle covers one, the X-ray window opens, and the eye has drifted.
4. **0:19** "That's binocular vision dysfunction, or BVD. Here's how it works, and a ten-second test you can try yourself." The title card. The promised test comes at 1:30 (the red dot), which keeps people watching.

**Why "20/20" instead of "your eye doctor says your eyes are fine":** it keeps the same surprise without suggesting another doctor missed something, which viewers (and the College's advertising rules) could read as a criticism of colleagues. It's also exactly true: 20/20 measures how clearly you see, not how well your eyes line up.

## Recording your voice

- Use a quiet, soft-furnished room and a phone or USB microphone about a hand's width from your mouth. Record each chapter as its own take; it's easier to redo one than the whole thing.
- The caption file (`bvd-video.srt`) works as a script you can follow, already split into sentences and timed.
- In a video editor (CapCut, iMovie, DaVinci Resolve or Premiere), put `bvd-video.mp4` on the first track and your takes underneath, starting each shot's line at the time in the script above. A quiet music bed is optional.
- If a line runs long, it's fine to slide the following lines a little later and trim the gaps; the animation is forgiving.
- Optional and effective: film yourself saying the first two lines to camera, then cut to the animation at "Watch this eye".

## Making it again

Serve the repository locally (for example `npx http-server -p 8080`), then:

```
node tools/record-video.mjs --url http://localhost:8080/video.html --out bvd-video.mp4
node tools/thumbnails.mjs --url http://localhost:8080/ --out thumbs/
```

`--from` and `--to` record part of the video (shots by number, `--to` not included), and `--format 9x16` records vertically. On a computer with a graphics card, recording takes a few minutes; without one, set `SWIFTSHADER=1` and allow about an hour. To preview without recording, open `video.html` in a browser and press **Play**; it plays in real time with the captions (you could also screen-record that directly).

## Accuracy notes

The video uses the website's wording rules: the link between eye alignment and symptoms is "thought to"; BVD is a common name rather than a single diagnosis; normal eye-muscle signals are separated from the extra from holding and correcting misaligned eyes; "switches off" for saccadic suppression is a simplification of a large, brief drop in visual sensitivity; and there are no comparisons with other practitioners.
