What you are seeing

  1. Saccade. The eyes jump together to the next target or word.
  2. Landing. If the eyes' resting position (their posture) is not perfectly aligned, they can land slightly out of line. This small error is called fixation disparity.
  3. Report. Stretch sensors (proprioceptors) in the eye muscles detect the error. It has been proposed that these signals travel through the trigeminal nerve, the nerve that also carries sensation from the face, forehead and jaw.
  4. Correction. The brain sends a small vergence movement to bring the eyes back into line, and the word is seen singly.

Reading a page can mean thousands of these corrections. The proposal, sometimes called trigeminal dysphoria, is that the constant reports add up and, together with other stressors such as weather changes, stress and poor sleep, may contribute to eye strain, headaches and neck tension. If the eyes rest in line, there is little to correct.

The cover test

Covering one eye takes away the need to keep the eyes aligned. Behind the paddle the covered eye drifts to its resting position: outward with an exo posture, inward with an eso posture. When the paddle is removed, the eye moves back to rejoin the other. Turn on "See through the paddle" to watch it happen.
