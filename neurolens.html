Why small misalignments can cause symptoms

1

The cause

To look at anything up close, like a phone, book or screen, both eyes turn slightly inward to meet on the same point. The eye muscles tell the brain where each eye is pointing. For many people this lining up is a little off, so the brain makes small corrections all day.

2

The effect

That eye-position information travels along the trigeminal nerve, which also carries feeling from the face and jaw and from the covering of the brain, and meets the nerves from the back of the head and neck in the brainstem. The idea behind Neurolens is that constant corrections over-stimulate this nerve, which may show up as headaches, eye strain, jaw tension, neck pain, a dry-eye feeling or motion sickness.

3

The solution

Prism in a glasses lens shifts what you see very slightly, so your eyes don't have to work as hard to line up. Eyes are usually further out of line up close than far away, so Neurolens uses a contoured prism: more toward the bottom of the lens, where you read, and less at the top. Neurolens is backed by a 100% satisfaction guarantee.

What about blue-light filters or the 20-20-20 rule? They can make screen time more comfortable, and newer lens designs can make screens sharper, but none of them change how well your eyes line up.
